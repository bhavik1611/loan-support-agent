Meridian Bank levies a late-payment fee on a credit card whenever the total amount due is not paid by the due date, and the late-payment fee is tiered by the size of that outstanding amount.
A Meridian cardholder with a total amount due up to 5,000 rupees is charged a late-payment fee of 400 rupees.
A Meridian cardholder with a total amount due between 5,001 and 20,000 rupees is charged a late-payment fee of 700 rupees.
A Meridian cardholder with a total amount due above 20,000 rupees is charged a late-payment fee of 1,200 rupees.
Once the full outstanding amount on a Meridian credit card is not paid, finance charges begin accruing from the original transaction date rather than from the due date.
A Meridian cardholder who carries a balance past the due date loses the interest-free period on purchases for the entire billing cycle, not only on the unpaid portion.
Meridian charges an overlimit fee of 2.5 percent of the amount by which the credit limit is exceeded, subject to a minimum of 600 rupees, and this overlimit fee applies only where the cardholder has separately opted in to the overlimit facility.
A payment that is returned unpaid on a Meridian credit card attracts a separate returned-payment charge of 450 rupees.
Meridian reports a late payment to the credit bureaus once it remains outstanding for more than 30 days past the due date.
Paying only the minimum amount due on a Meridian credit card avoids the late-payment fee but does not avoid the finance charge on the remaining balance.
Meridian will reverse one late-payment fee as a goodwill gesture, and a cardholder can receive this reversal only once in any rolling 12-month period.
Meridian expects cardholders to track both the late-payment fee tiers and the overlimit consent requirement, since the two charges apply independently of each other.
