Meridian Bank distinguishes foreclosure from part-prepayment as two different ways a borrower can pay down a loan ahead of schedule.
Foreclosure at Meridian Bank means repaying the entire outstanding balance in one payment, and foreclosure closes the loan account completely.
Part-prepayment at Meridian Bank means repaying only a portion of the outstanding balance, and the loan account continues after a part-prepayment.
When a Meridian borrower makes a part-prepayment, the tenure is shortened by default while the instalment amount stays the same.
A Meridian borrower may instead ask that a part-prepayment reduce the instalment amount rather than the tenure, and Meridian will apply that preference on request.
Meridian sets a minimum part-prepayment amount of 25,000 rupees or one instalment, whichever is higher, and allows at most 4 part-prepayments in a financial year.
A Meridian borrower who wants to foreclose a loan must first obtain a foreclosure statement from the bank, and that foreclosure statement stays valid for 15 days.
Meridian returns the original property documents to the borrower within 30 days of a foreclosure being completed.
Meridian updates the credit bureaus within 30 days of a loan being closed through foreclosure.
Both foreclosure and part-prepayment at Meridian Bank can attract a charge, and that charge itself is set out in the prepayment charge schedule rather than in this comparison.
A Meridian borrower deciding between foreclosure and part-prepayment should weigh the loss of the loan facility against the smaller ongoing saving a part-prepayment produces.
Meridian treats the two prepayment routes as distinct transactions for processing purposes even when a borrower requests both a part-prepayment and, later, a foreclosure on the same loan.
